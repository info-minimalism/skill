# Publisher self-certification

**Version:** 1.0.0

## What the mark means

The mark is a publisher's public promise: from an identified adoption date, check every new in-scope final publication before release with the latest published Standard, and resolve findings under P1.

It is not a certificate issued by the agent. A one-time channel sample does not prove the promise has been kept. The expected exchange is reader loyalty; it is not guaranteed audience growth.

Use ordinary publishing and feedback tools. No registry, outside approver, periodic renewal, or formal appeal process is required.

## Before each publication

1. **Check the version.** Follow [release verification](release-verification.md). An installed copy is not evidence that it is latest.
2. **Freeze the finished content.** Record its exact text or accessible capture, identifier, evaluation date, audience, encounter, and version. Use message, sequence, or channel context as needed. Check preceding posts when duplication, repeated updates, or artificial fragmentation is relevant; do not isolate a post to hide a recurring burden.
3. **Evaluate and resolve.** Apply P1: no unresolved high- or medium-confidence demonstrated avoidable cost, preserved meaning and function, no needed local-context repair, and no material unresolved uncertainty. Record the reason for each accepted, resolved, or rejected finding. Rejecting an edit requires evidence of necessary meaning or function, not simply publisher preference.
4. **Check the actual final version.** If it changed after evaluation, evaluate the edited content. A proposed rewrite is not evidence that the published version was checked.
5. **Keep the evidence.** Retain a dated, version-pinned record tied to that final content, with relevant context and findings. Make supporting evaluations available through a link or ordinary request route; a linked sample may illustrate the routine but cannot replace checks of the other publications.

An evaluator can recommend that the inspected final content meets the pinned version's requirements. The publisher owns the continuing declaration. Do not claim to have verified every past or future publication from a sample.

If release information is unavailable, still offer a pinned-version evaluation, but do not confirm that it fulfills the latest-version commitment. State the missing check rather than creating a zero, a pass, or a permanent failure.

## Declaration template

> **Info-Minimalism — publisher self-certified**
>
> From [adoption date], we commit to checking every new in-scope post on [site/channel] before publication using the latest published Info-Minimalism Standard, and resolving its supported findings. This is our commitment, not an independent certificate or a guarantee of truth.
>
> Standard and skill: https://github.com/info-minimalism/skill  
> Supporting evaluations: [link or how to request them]  
> Feedback: [existing contact or comment route]

The mark or bio should link to this declaration, or include its substance where a separate page is impractical. Identify the source clearly. The archive before adoption is not automatically covered. Do not imply that a checkmark on a page certifies unrelated sites or all content on its platform.

When testing rather than making a real declaration, prefix the template **“TEST ONLY — not a live certification”**. Drafting it is not permission to post it.

## Check a publisher's claim

Read the actual declaration, covered source, and adoption date. Inspect the evidence available and report only what it supports:

- An item record supports a finding about that dated version of that item.
- A sample supports observations about that sample.
- Evidence of a missed check or unresolved supported finding can challenge adherence to the continuing promise.
- Missing public evidence alone does not prove that no check occurred. Ask for the relevant record through the ordinary feedback route if the user wants to pursue it.
- A record using an older Standard may be valid history. Compare its release date with the publication time; do not retroactively impose today's version on yesterday's publication.

Do not infer ongoing compliance from a clean sample, nor wrongdoing from the absence of a mark. Label publisher self-evaluation accurately. Agents run by the publisher are not independent reviewers merely because their contexts differ.

## Objections and missed checks

Read the actual content, encounter assumptions, and objection. A concrete necessity argument may overturn a proposed cut; a supported avoidable burden needs correction and a fresh check of the final content. Don't settle semantic disagreements by agent vote.

If a post skipped its pre-publication check, checking it now can repair the content but cannot make the original timing compliant. Record the lapse honestly and correct the routine. If the publisher cannot maintain the every-publication commitment, recommend removing the mark until it can. Do not remove or alter a live mark without authorization.

Readers may give honest feedback, ask questions, or leave a source. They are not obliged to perform audits. Do not promise platform enforcement, automatic ranking changes, or independent assurance that does not exist.
