# Mode Procedures

**Applies to:** Info-Minimalism Standard v1.0.0

## Common procedure

1. Freeze the observed content and assign an evaluation ID.
2. Declare mode, boundary, platform, encounter context, audience, supplied context, and cost method.
3. Map expressed payload and material qualifications.
4. Record preservation constraints, including any need for independent shareability, local context, accessibility equivalence, search retrieval, or ordered learning.
5. Construct a minimal adequate rendering.
6. Check MAR fidelity and record plausible losses or alternative interpretations.
7. Annotate removable burden and adequacy gaps.
8. Calculate required and applicable metrics.
9. State uncertainty, limitations, and the version-pinned outcome; distinguish evaluation from a publisher's continuing commitment.

Do not read engagement performance as evidence that wording was necessary. Do not infer intent from a pattern that can be described directly.

For each accepted transformation, record the meaning-ledger entries affected, the reduced cost base, any burden transferred to another base, and preservation confidence. Prefer one MAR only when it reduces at least one cost without materially increasing another; otherwise retain multiple non-dominated alternatives.

## Message mode

Use the complete consumer-visible message. Include captions, embedded text, and mandatory link labels that are part of the immediate encounter. Evaluate linked destinations separately unless following the link is necessary to receive the promised payload and is explicitly included in the boundary.

For a reply or quoted post, include the minimum parent context needed to resolve the reply. If the parent is unavailable, constrain or abstain.

## Sequence mode

Freeze the full intended sequence and its order. Record:

- number of parts;
- platform limits known to affect splitting;
- whether consumers can preview the whole sequence;
- interaction steps or attentive seconds required to traverse it;
- payload first introduced in each part;
- orientation or recap repeated because of the split.

Construct both a sequence-preserving MAR and, when practical, a consolidated MAR. The difference supports fragmentation analysis. Do not penalize multipart structure that improves accessibility, comprehension, progressive action, or update timing.

Report both local item findings and global sequence findings. Efficient items can form an inefficient sequence, and duplicated orientation may remain necessary when parts are plausibly encountered independently.

## Channel mode

Define a sampling rule before evaluation. Record the time window, item count, exclusions, deletions visible to the evaluator, and whether the sample is complete. A convenience sample may be diagnostic but cannot support claims about the whole channel.

Map recurring propositions across items, separating:

- genuinely new or changed payload;
- necessary standalone orientation;
- useful reminders or spaced-learning recurrence;
- unchanged duplicate material;
- capture and navigation overhead.

Recurrence or timing is a preservation constraint only when supported by observable evidence in the artifact–audience–context relation, such as an explicit reminder purpose, deadline or urgency, spaced-learning design, independently encountered items, or a declared audience task. The generic possibility that a recipient may forget does not by itself exempt unchanged recurrence. When evidence is absent, record reminder value as a counterinterpretation and use confidence appropriate to the encounter model; when encounter evidence is missing and outcome-changing, report conditional or partial results.

Analyze at least two plausible encounter models when results depend on whether consumers read every item or encounter items in isolation. Do not convert a message result into a channel result.

Channel claims describe the sample unless a predeclared sampling design supports a wider inference. Report distributions and recurring patterns rather than averaging item results into a fictional typical post.

## Partial and insufficient evaluations

Use `partial` only when the exact evaluated boundary is frozen and some findings remain stable across reasonable context interpretations or inspectable modalities. A partial record must name the assessable layer and withhold every unsupported MAR or metric.

Use `insufficient_context` when the payload, material referents, artifact wording, unit boundary, or encounter model cannot be resolved well enough to construct a faithful MAR for the requested layer. A semantic summary can support corpus design, but cannot support a wording-level evaluation. Structural sequence or channel findings require exact events and boundaries even when wording metrics are withheld.
